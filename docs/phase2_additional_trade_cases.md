# Phase 2 provincial generation declines and trade

This comparison screens annual provincial generation for the largest year-over-year decline in a single source in Ontario, Alberta, Nova Scotia, and Newfoundland and Labrador from 2016 to 2025. It also includes Ontario’s largest nuclear decline and Newfoundland and Labrador’s preceding hydro decline.

| Ontario 2021 | Nuclear | −4.90 | −1.26 | +1.92 | −3.50 | +5.42 | −0.74 |
| Alberta 2020 | Combustible fuels | −7.25 | +1.13 | +2.37 | −0.28 | +2.65 | −3.47 |

| Newfoundland and Labrador 2025 | Hydro | −5.14 | +0.14 | +0.02 | −5.95 | +5.97 | +0.97 |

All values are changes from the previous calendar year in million MWh. Other local generation is the change in total generation minus the affected source’s change. Net receipts equal receipts minus deliveries. Available electricity equals local generation plus net receipts in these cases. Each balance uses 12 months per year and reconciles before rounding. Positive changes in net receipts can occur while a province remains a net exporter.

## Ontario 2017

Other local generation and lower outbound deliveries helped offset the combustible generation decline. IESO reports that Ontario demand fell 4.9 TWh, transmission-connected gas and oil output fell from 12.7 to 5.9 TWh, and hydro output rose from 35.7 to 37.7 TWh. IESO’s market series shows falling gross imports, while the Statistics Canada provincial balance shows rising gross receipts. Compare coverage and definitions before interpreting the difference. Keep Ontario 2021 as a separate nuclear comparison.

## Alberta 2020

Higher receipts and other local generation offset part of the combustible generation decline. Electricity available for use fell by 3.47 million MWh. The drivers of the generation and trade changes remain open.

## Nova Scotia 2023

Nova Scotia Power reported receiving 819,815 MWh more from Nalcor via the Maritime Link in 2023, close to the 842,064 MWh increase in provincial gross receipts. Compare full-year actuals in the two Q4 fuel adjustment reports, then align monthly thermal generation and Maritime Link receipts. Check the audit for dispatch and emissions-pricing changes and the board decision for the timing of the Trenton Unit 5 outage. The annual balance leaves each factor’s contribution to lower thermal generation open.

## Newfoundland and Labrador 2025

Lower outbound deliveries more than offset the hydro decline. Hydro-Québec reported Churchill Falls purchases of 22.2 TWh in 2025 versus 27.1 TWh in 2024, a 4.9 TWh change alongside a 5.95 TWh reduction in province-wide deliveries. These series have different coverage. Check whether the March 2024 banked-energy agreement raised the comparison year and use monthly station generation, reservoir inflows or levels, and unit outage dates to test operational explanations. Note 22(c) in the Churchill Falls statements describes the water management agreement with Muskrat Falls.
