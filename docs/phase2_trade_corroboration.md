# Phase 2 Corroboration: Provincial Generation and Trade Responses

## British Columbia: hydro weakness and increased reliance on electricity trade

British Columbia provides one of the clearest examples of electricity trade helping absorb a decline in domestic generation. Our analysis shows that total provincial generation fell by approximately **12.7 million MWh in 2023**, while net electricity receipts increased by approximately **16.6 million MWh**. This shift reflected both an increase of about **9.4 million MWh in receipts** and a reduction of roughly **7.2 million MWh in deliveries**.

External evidence supports the interpretation that hydroelectric conditions were central to this shift. Statistics Canada reports that British Columbia's hydroelectric generation fell **22.8% in 2023**, primarily because of drought conditions, while parts of the province experienced record-low precipitation and abnormally low reservoir levels. At the same time, BC became Canada's largest electricity importer, importing **16.8 million MWh from the United States**, while its electricity exports declined sharply.

Statistics Canada also reports that BC electricity imports climbed substantially during periods of weak hydro generation in 2023, reaching record levels during the summer and early fall. The agency notes that the gap between provincial generation and consumption contributed to unusually high imports during the drought.

Taken together, the evidence is consistent with **drought-related hydro weakness reducing BC's domestic electricity availability and export capacity, with the province responding through increased electricity receipts and reduced deliveries**. The data do not establish that hydro conditions were the sole cause of the trade shift, but the timing and external evidence strongly support this interpretation.

**Sources**
- Statistics Canada, *Dry weather dampens overall generation of electricity: Year in review, 2023*: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/241022/dq241022e-eng.htm
- Statistics Canada, *Extreme weather by the numbers: How weather impacts energy output*: https://www.statcan.gc.ca/o1/en/plus/7628-extreme-weather-numbers-how-weather-impacts-energy-output

## Quebec: hydro decline primarily reduced export capacity

Quebec shows a related but somewhat different response. Our analysis indicates that provincial generation fell by approximately **20.2 million MWh in 2023**, while electricity deliveries declined by roughly **13.2 million MWh** and net receipts increased by approximately **15.2 million MWh**. Generation fell by another **10.1 million MWh in 2024**, accompanied by a further **9.2 million MWh decline in deliveries**.

Statistics Canada reports that Quebec's hydroelectric generation declined **9.7% in 2023**, or approximately **19.6 million MWh**, as drought affected hydroelectric production across much of Canada. Quebec's electricity exports to the United States also fell sharply. Statistics Canada reports a **41.1% year-over-year decline in Quebec exports** in 2023 and states that the province reduced exports in order to maintain water levels in hydroelectric reservoirs.

Hydro-Quebec's 2023 reporting provides additional context by showing a system that combines domestic hydroelectric generation, electricity purchases, and exports while serving the Quebec market and external customers.

The evidence therefore suggests that Quebec responded to weaker hydro conditions primarily through **reduced electricity deliveries and export capacity, supplemented by greater net receipts**, rather than by fully replacing lost hydroelectric production with another domestic generation technology. As with British Columbia, this should be treated as a strongly supported interpretation rather than a claim that drought alone explains every change in Quebec's electricity balance.

**Sources**
- Statistics Canada, *Dry weather dampens overall generation of electricity: Year in review, 2023*: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/241022/dq241022e-eng.htm
- Hydro-Quebec, *Annual Report 2023*: https://www.hydroquebec.com/data/documents-donnees/pdf/annual-report-2023-hydro-quebec.pdf

## Analytical approach

These case studies follow a consistent structure:

1. Use the project dataset to establish what changed in generation and trade.
2. Use external institutional sources to corroborate the conditions or mechanisms that may explain the observed pattern.
3. State the interpretation cautiously, distinguishing a supported association from a fully established causal claim.
