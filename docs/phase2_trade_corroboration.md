# Phase 2 Corroboration: Provincial Generation and Trade Responses

## British Columbia: hydro weakness and increased reliance on electricity trade

British Columbia provides one of the clearest examples of electricity trade helping absorb a decline in domestic generation. Our analysis shows that total provincial generation fell by approximately **12.7 million MWh in 2023**, while net electricity receipts increased by approximately **16.6 million MWh**. This shift reflected both a rise of about **9.4 million MWh in gross receipts** and a reduction of roughly **7.2 million MWh in deliveries**.

External evidence supports the interpretation that hydroelectric conditions were central to this shift. Statistics Canada reports that British Columbia's hydroelectric generation fell **22.8% in 2023**, primarily because of drought conditions, while parts of the province experienced record-low precipitation and abnormally low reservoir levels. At the same time, BC became Canada's largest electricity importer, importing a total of **16.8 million MWh from the United States** over the year, while its electricity exports declined sharply. This annual US-import total is not directly comparable to the **9.4 million MWh year-over-year increase in gross receipts** in our provincial balance data, because the two measures are defined differently; the project therefore treats the StatCan import figure as corroborating context rather than as a component that should reconcile exactly to the receipts series.

Statistics Canada notes that imports climbed substantially during periods of weak hydro generation, reaching record levels during the summer and early fall, as the gap between provincial generation and consumption widened.

Taken together, the evidence is consistent with **drought-related hydro weakness reducing BC's domestic electricity availability and export capacity, with the province responding through increased electricity receipts and reduced deliveries**. The data do not establish that hydro conditions were the sole cause of the trade shift, but the timing and external evidence strongly support this interpretation.

**Sources**
- Statistics Canada, *Dry weather dampens overall generation of electricity: Year in review, 2023*: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/241022/dq241022e-eng.htm
- Statistics Canada, *Extreme weather by the numbers: How weather impacts energy output*: https://www.statcan.gc.ca/o1/en/plus/7628-extreme-weather-numbers-how-weather-impacts-energy-output

## Quebec: hydro decline primarily reduced export capacity

Quebec shows a related but somewhat different response. Our analysis indicates that provincial generation fell by approximately **20.2 million MWh in 2023**, of which roughly **19.6 million MWh, or 9.7%,** was attributable to declining hydroelectric output, consistent with the drought conditions affecting hydroelectric production across much of Canada. Over the same period, electricity deliveries declined by roughly **13.2 million MWh**, while net receipts increased by approximately **15.2 million MWh**. In 2024, generation fell by a further **10.1 million MWh**, deliveries declined by an additional **9.2 million MWh**, and net receipts increased by approximately **11.2 million MWh**.

Quebec's electricity exports to the United States also fell sharply. Statistics Canada reports a **41.1% year-over-year decline in Quebec exports in 2023** and states that the province reduced exports in order to maintain water levels in hydroelectric reservoirs.

The evidence therefore suggests that Quebec responded to weaker hydro conditions primarily through **reduced electricity deliveries and export capacity, supplemented by greater net receipts**, rather than by fully replacing lost hydroelectric production with another domestic generation technology. As with British Columbia, this should be treated as a strongly supported interpretation rather than a claim that drought alone explains every change in Quebec's electricity balance.

**Sources**
- Statistics Canada, *Dry weather dampens overall generation of electricity: Year in review, 2023*: https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/241022/dq241022e-eng.htm

## Analytical approach

These case studies follow a consistent structure:

1. Use the project dataset to establish what changed in generation and trade.
2. Use external institutional sources to corroborate the conditions or mechanisms that may explain the observed pattern.
3. State the interpretation cautiously, distinguishing a supported association from a fully established causal claim.
